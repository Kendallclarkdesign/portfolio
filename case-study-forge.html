TL;DR

FORGE Fitness is a fictional gym I built as sample work for Towpath Creative, targeting gym and studio owners as prospective clients. What makes it worth a case study isn't the fictional brand, it's the process: three complete visual redesigns in one continuous session, each one a full rebuild of the design system, not a palette swap, plus real licensed photography, and accessibility fixes caught and corrected along the way. It's a demonstration of range and speed, not a finished product pretending to be client work.

The Brief I Gave Myself

Towpath Creative needed a strong sample project aimed at gym owners, the kind of prospect who wants to see a site that looks like it belongs to a real, modern gym before they'll trust an agency with their own brand. A generic template wouldn't sell that. It needed to look purpose-built.

I started from a reference screenshot of an existing gym template for layout inspiration, then diverged from it fully once real content, a real palette, and real photography came in. The goal was never to copy a template, it was to prove I could take a rough visual direction and turn it into something original and production-ready.

Three Redesigns, Not One Polish Pass

Most portfolio pieces show one finished design. FORGE went through three, each a genuine rebuild:

01V1: black-and-red "gym template" aesthetic, Bebas Neue display type, sharp-edged components, built directly from the reference layout
02V2: refined to a deep forest-green and warm gold palette after a color-contrast audit flagged real WCAG failures in V1's accent color against white text
03V3: full system overhaul, a floating pill navigation, alternating light and dark sections, rounded cards, a bright lime accent, and real trainer photography replacing every placeholder

Each version kept the same underlying HTML structure and copy, only the design system changed. That's what let three full redesigns happen in one session instead of three separate projects.

Services page, final design: real program photography, dark pricing section with a highlighted plan

Sourcing Photography That Would Survive Scrutiny

Every photo on the final site, six trainer portraits, three program shots, two facility images, is a real licensed photo. Sourcing them meant vetting each one against the brief:

  • Rejected a coach photo with a competing gym's logo clearly visible on a wall sign in the background
  • Rejected a shirt with visible third-party branding on another candidate trainer photo
  • Excluded a premium Unsplash+ photo that came up in results but isn't free to use
  • Verified every image resolved before committing to it, rather than trusting a search result blind

The result is a "team" of six coaches who look consistent, professional, and specific to this brand, not generic stock filler.

Team section, six licensed photos, each individually vetted for background branding and licensing

Accessibility Caught in Review, Not After Launch

Two real contrast failures shipped in earlier versions and got caught before this write-up, not after:

  • White button text on a gold accent measured 2.3:1 contrast, WCAG AA requires 4.5:1 for body text. Fixed by switching to dark text on the accent instead of assuming white always works on a "bright" color.
  • The final lime accent used as link and label text on white backgrounds measured 1.7:1, worse than the first failure. A second, darker shade of the same hue was built specifically for text-on-light use, keeping the bright accent for buttons and dark backgrounds only.

Also built in from the start: visible focus rings that adapt between light and dark sections, 44px minimum touch targets on mobile controls, and a prefers-reduced-motion override. None of it is decorative, all of it is testable.

Why No Framework

FORGE is plain HTML, CSS, and JavaScript, no React, no build step, no dependencies. For agency sample work that needs to be forked into a new client vertical next week, that's a feature, not a limitation. Anyone can open the files and understand them immediately, and the whole thing deploys to Netlify by dragging a folder.

Reflection

The interesting part of this project isn't the final gym website, it's that the final version bears almost no resemblance to the first one, and that's the point. Client direction changes. Being able to take "change the colors" or "make it feel different" and deliver a genuinely different, coherent system, fast, is the actual skill being demonstrated here.

"Three redesigns in one session isn't indecision. It's proof the structure underneath was solid enough to carry any design on top of it."
